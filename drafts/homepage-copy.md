# Role.via homepage copy (draft)

**Status:** Draft for review. Nothing on the live site changes until this copy is moved into `src/` and built.
**Source of facts:** `.agents/product-marketing.md` (v2), the current site in `src/`, `plans.json` and `README.md`.
**Audience:** Indian campus placement candidates (final-year students and freshers).

**Diagnosis (copywriting skill, situation B):** The current homepage already has a voice: plain, strict and honest about its limits ("Speaking more is not rewarded", "Role.via shows no sample scores"). This draft keeps that voice and every fact, writes for placement season, and moves the strongest differences (the follow-up questions, CV questions, quoted evidence) higher up the page.

**Ground rules followed:** no user numbers, quotes, ratings or college names; no comparison between Premium and Placement Pro; brand written as "Role.via".

### 2. Company search

**Label:** Which company are you interviewing with?
**Placeholder:** Try Deloitte, HUL, TCS or a startup
**Button:** Search
**Helper text:** Popular companies autocomplete. Any other name is researched when you search, and every fact is labelled verified, reported or AI-generated.

### 3. The problem

**Heading:** Placement season gives you one shot at each company

**Body:**
The shortlist comes out on Monday. The GD is on Wednesday, and the interviews start that afternoon. Your placement cell can fit in a mock or two. Friends will ask the standard questions and nod at whatever you say.

Then the panel asks a follow-up you never rehearsed, about a project on your own CV.

Role.via asks you that follow-up while you can still fix the answer.

### 4. Two ways to practise

**Section heading:** Two rounds you can practise tonight

**Card 1: AI mock interview**
**Heading:** A spoken interview, one question at a time
**Body:** Pick a company, a role and a round: HR, technical, case, managerial or final. The interviewer speaks each question, listens to your answer, and follows up when the answer is thin. A separate situational and case round covers workplace scenarios and case questions.
**CTA:** Set up a mock interview

**Card 2: GD practice**
**Heading:** Group-discussion practice with strict feedback
**Body:** Get a topic, such as "Should freshers be paid based on skills or degrees?", and speak on it. Your score covers relevance, listening and balance, with quotes from what you said. Talking longer earns you nothing. Topics come from categories or from any company you name.
**CTA:** Start a GD practice

### 5. What the questions are built from

**Section heading:** Questions built from the company, the role and your CV

**The company**
Search any company, including startups. You get its business model, products, competitors, stated values and hiring process where known. Each item says where it came from: verified, reported or AI-generated.

**The role**
Choose Marketing, Finance, Consulting, Sales, Product Management, Operations, Technology, HR, or Data & Analytics. A Finance mock and a Marketing mock at the same company ask different questions.

**Your CV (optional)**
Upload your CV and the interviewer reads it before the first question. Expect questions like "Your CV has a gap between June and December. What were you doing in that time?" and "What exactly was your own role in this project?"

### 6. The report

**Heading:** A report that shows its working

**Body:**
After each mock you get scores for communication and clarity, relevance and depth, structured thinking, role knowledge and problem solving. Each score points to the lines from your answers that earned it, along with a suggestion for a stronger answer. Your progress charts and revision plan update from your saved sessions.

**Small print under the report preview:** These are layout previews. Role.via shows no sample scores, because every number in your account comes from your own sessions.

### 7. How it works

**Heading:** From company name to report in four steps

1. **Search the company.** Any name works, including startups.
2. **Pick the role and round.** Add your CV if you want questions about your own projects.
3. **Practise out loud.** You'll need a microphone and Chrome, Edge or Safari.
4. **Read the report.** Fix the weak answers, then run the round again.

**CTA:** Start my free mock interview

### 9. Straight answers (FAQ)

**Will Role.via tell me if I'll get shortlisted?**
No. Your scores measure how you did in practice. Role.via doesn't predict hiring decisions and doesn't guarantee a shortlist.

**How accurate is the company information?**
Every item has a label. "Verified" and "reported" items come from sources. "AI-generated" means no verified source was found, so the AI wrote it from general knowledge. Check those items before you repeat them to a panel.

**I'm a fresher. Is this for me?**
Yes. Choose "Student / Fresher" and the questions match your level. Role.via also covers 0–2, 3–6 and 7+ years of experience.

**What do I need?**
A microphone, speakers or headphones, and Chrome, Edge or Safari. Interviews are voice-only.

**Where does my CV go?**
Your reports, transcripts and CV stay in your browser. When you use an AI feature, the text it needs (company, role, your answers and your CV text) is sent to Google's Gemini API, and Google may use free-tier data to improve its products. The Privacy Policy has the details.

**Is this a subscription?**
No. Packs are one-time purchases, and 30-day passes end on their own. Checkout runs in rupees.

## Annotations

- **Hero:** Follows discomfort (the panel), then path (name the company and role), then proof of difference (follow-ups and quoted evidence). The tagline "Know the company. Prepare for the role. Own the room." moves to the footer because it reads as a slogan. The H1 now says what the product does.
- **Problem section:** A scene students will recognise instead of a statistic, because no verified numbers exist. It is the page's one punchy moment.
- **CV example questions:** Taken from the question templates in `src/iv.html`, so they're real behaviour. Concrete questions are more convincing than "personalised questions".
- **Report section:** Keeps the site's existing honesty line about sample scores. With no proof yet, showing how the scoring works is the strongest credibility the page has.
- **Pricing preview:** Rupees first because checkout runs in INR and the audience is Indian; dollars kept in brackets to match the current pricing page. Gives the starting price of the passes only, so Premium and Placement Pro are never set side by side. The internal note "Proposed prices to test, not validated market rates" is removed.
- **FAQ:** Answers the four worries this audience has: false promises, made-up company facts, whether freshers fit, and privacy. Each answer is taken from the Help page, the About page or the in-app privacy notice.
- **No social proof section:** Left out on purpose. See below for what would unlock one.
